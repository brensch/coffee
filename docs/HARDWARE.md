# Hardware

Target: **Gaggia Classic Pro E24, US 120 V / 60 Hz** (brass boiler). For why this machine, see the
README. This page covers:
- what you buy;
- the rules the hardware follows;
- how it's wired;
- the enclosure.

The board itself (schematic as code, calculations, simulations) is in [CIRCUIT.md](CIRCUIT.md).
Fitting it is in [INSTALL.md](INSTALL.md), and measuring the machine for the models is in
[CALIBRATION.md](CALIBRATION.md).

## Design rules

1. **Mains only where it's been designed for.** The board switches the loads itself. The only
   crossings between mains and low voltage are certified isolating parts (three opto-triacs and
   the AC-DC module), across a 7 mm copper-free band with milled slots. A code check fails the
   build if any net crosses the barrier, and a DRC rule set (the "mains lint") enforces 2 mm
   between mains nets.
2. **Hardware protection stays in series with the heater.** Keep the stock steam thermostat (or
   fit a 165–170 °C manual-reset limiter) and the thermal fuse. Software can only *ask* a
   failed-short triac to stop (scenario 12).
3. **Whole mains cycles only.** The engine never does phase-angle control. Zero-cross opto-triacs
   switch all three loads, including the pump. That's why no zero-cross detector, dimmer module
   or phase timing is needed: the firmware ticks once per cycle from its crystal.
4. **The firmware does what it can; the board keeps only physics.** The board does isolation,
   switching and sensor-to-voltage. Debounce, calibration, linearisation, fault detection,
   oversampling and timing are Rust.
5. **Measure what the model needs.** Each sensor maps to model parameters. Anything we can't
   sense is estimated, and the UI says so.

Every part was checked against two questions: is it rated for a 70 °C ambient with margin, and what wears out?

## Wiring (summary)

| Load | Path |
|---|---|
| Heater | live → stock steam thermostat → thermal fuse → **J2 (board triac, in place of the brew thermostat)** → element → neutral |
| Pump | live → **J3.2 (board triac + snubber)** → pump → neutral |
| 3-way valve | live → **J3.1 (board triac + snubber)** → valve → neutral |
| Board supply | **J1**: L and N after the power switch |
| Brew / steam switches | disconnected from mains, rewired as dry contacts to **J7** |

The full table, pigtails and pre-power checks are in INSTALL.md §5.

## Enclosure
- **Where:** inside the Classic's case, on the side panel, as far from the boiler as possible.
  The heater triac's tab bolts to the metal chassis, which is its heatsink.
- **Material:** PC, or V-0 rated PETG/ASA. PLA softens at ~55 °C, and the case interior reaches
  60–70 °C near the boiler.
- **Design:** parametric OpenSCAD around the board (56 × 52 mm, no mounting holes: held by its edges in grooves, plus the
  triac tab). It needs:
  - edge grooves (no standoffs: the board has no holes; leave 3 mm under it for the bottom-side parts);
  - a separate clip-on cover over the mains half;
  - strain relief for the sensor leads;
  - vents;
  - a slot for the USB-C, used only for flashing;
  - an exit for the antenna pigtail.
- **Still needed:** measurements of the free space inside your case, and where the side panel can
  take the triac bolt.
